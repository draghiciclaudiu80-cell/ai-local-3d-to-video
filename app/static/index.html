Reasoning

For questions with sizes, angles, distances, counts, dates or logic, the AI doesn't guess the number: it writes the problem as a small program (every quantity named, shapes and positions as coordinates), runs it in the sandbox, re-runs it with changed numbers to check it really works it out (the “perturbation test”), and answers from the result — a 📐 card shows the working. About 10-30 s more on such questions; pictures, 3D, apps and normal talk aren't affected. It can't change how a model thinks inside (that is done when a model is trained); it makes its answers rest on computation.

Laya — fast decisions (the real Laya)

        
        
Laya (Convai Innovations, open source, Apache 2.0) is a 421M decision model — System 1. It never writes text:
          it reads your message and decides what you want (a picture, a 3D model, a search, just talking…) in a fraction of a second on the
          processor, with how sure it is. Sure → it acts. Not sure → the main model decides, or you get buttons to choose — and every
          button you click teaches it how you say things. English uses the fine-tuned decision model, Romanian and other languages
          the multilingual one. It uses ~1–2 GB of RAM while it works and stops after 10 idle minutes.

        

      
Security
The rule: the AI may read untrusted information (web pages, files, tool results), but untrusted information can never give it authority. The AI only proposes — this app decides, and you approve anything risky.

Internet

        
        
        
Searches, pages, pictures and videos go through the Tor network: websites don't see your address,
          each search takes a new route, and if Tor isn't connected nothing is sent. The app's window itself never goes online.
